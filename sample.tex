Dynamical-Mass Bias and Required Response Fidelity}

\abstract{
Treating a luminous unresolved binary as a flux-weighted photocentre is convenient, but in the marginal-resolution regime the measured one-dimensional position can depend nonlinearly on component separation, light ratio, and scan orientation. We investigate how this measurement-model choice propagates into joint dynamical inference when Gaia-like along-scan astrometry is combined with independent resolved relative astrometry and both radial-velocity curves of an SB2. Three response models are compared on identical synthetic realizations: $\mzero$, an ordinary photocentre; $\mone$, the published equal-width one-dimensional blended-profile response; and $\mtwo$, a finite-elongation orientation-dependent Gaussian response based on the effective-width treatment of \citet{Penoyre2026}. The response widths are research-surrogate parameters, not calibrated Gaia PLSF values.
A 720-fit response-fidelity grid spans three Gaia-band light fractions, four values of $\aalpha$, two elongations $\psfelong$, ten noise realizations, and all three fitted models. At $\betag=0.25$, $\aalpha=1$, and $\psfelong=1.5$, the photocentre model biases the median inferred masses by $-1.26\%$ and $-1.28\%$, the parallax by $+0.93\%$, and the light fraction by $-15.34\%$. The equal-width response reduces the corresponding median mass biases to $-0.03\%$ and $-0.09\%$, although it remains measurably misspecified relative to $\mtwo$ (${\rm median}\,[\chi^2_{\mone}-\chi^2_{\mtwo}]=129.3$) and retains a $-3.30\%$ light-fraction bias.
A 270-fit control then weakens only the independent resolved-astrometry/SB2 precision. The photocentre mass biases grow from about $1.2\%$ under strong external constraints to $5$--$6\%$ under weak constraints, while the equal-width response develops residual mass biases of about $1$--$1.4\%$. The correctly specified $\mtwo$ solution remains centered on the injected parameters, with increasing random scatter as the external information weakens. A separate 100-seed matched-$\mtwo$ control gives a median parallax bias of only $+0.017\%$ with a 16--84 percentile range of $-0.127\%$ to $+0.178\%$.
The response fidelity required for accurate dynamical masses therefore depends on the strength of independent orbital constraints. Light ratio is more sensitive to response misspecification than the component masses in strongly constrained visual-SB2 systems. Supporting full-sky baseline experiments further show that the strength of model mismatch depends strongly on the number and geometry of Gaia-like scans.

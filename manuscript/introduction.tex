\section{Introduction}
\label{sect:intro}

Binary-star orbits are among the principal routes to dynamical stellar masses. Combining independent relative astrometry and spectroscopy is especially powerful because the sky-plane orbit constrains geometry while a double-lined spectroscopic binary (SB2) provides both line-of-sight velocity amplitudes. Gaia adds precise one-dimensional astrometric information over a mission-length time baseline \citep{GaiaMission2016}.

The broad concept of combining Gaia or Hipparcos astrometry with relative astrometry and radial velocities is established. BINARYS jointly treats Hipparcos/Gaia absolute astrometry with relative astrometry and radial velocities, including resolved cases \citep{Leclerc2023}, while orvara provides posterior inference from radial velocity, absolute astrometry, and relative astrometry \citep{Brandt2021}. The present problem is therefore narrower. Gaia's published DR3 astrometric-binary processing explicitly applied preliminary filtering to remove partially resolved double stars from the orbital pipeline \citep{Halbwachs2023}, and \citet{Holl2023} demonstrated that close source structure can introduce scan-angle-dependent image-parameter biases.

A nonlinear scan-angle- and separation-dependent blended-source response is also already established. \citet{ElBadry2024} released \texttt{gaiamock}, whose \texttt{al\_bias\_binary} places the measured one-dimensional coordinate at the peak of the combined along-scan flux profile. Our equal-width Gaussian implementation reproduces that published response over their common single-peak domain, so we make no claim to new measurement physics. Moreover, \citet{Penoyre2026} derived the more general position and resolvability problem for blended Gaussian point sources, including an elongated Gaia-like PSF whose effective width depends on orientation. The constant-width response used in the baseline experiments below must therefore be understood as a controlled restricted case of a broader published blended-source framework.

Nor is Bayesian inference from Gaia epoch astrometry plus radial velocities itself an open problem. \citet{Baycroft2026} introduced Gaia epoch-astrometry models in \texttt{kima} and demonstrated Bayesian analysis of Gaia epoch astrometry both alone and jointly with radial velocities. The remaining question investigated here is at the intersection of these established capabilities.

We therefore test a \emph{candidate inference gap}: fitting a resolution-aware blended-source response inside a target-level orbit likelihood while simultaneously constraining the same physical orbit with independent resolved relative astrometry and both SB2 velocity curves, and propagating measurement-model assumptions into individual component masses, parallax, and Gaia-band light fraction. We do not claim priority for this combination; the literature search is recorded explicitly in \texttt{docs/literature\_gap.md} so that the claim can be narrowed further if a direct precedent is found.

The immediate goal is not a calibrated Gaia image model. Gaia's real PSF/LSF is time-, colour-, focal-plane-, and instrument-state dependent \citep{Rowell2021,Rowell2026}. In particular, the DR4 PSF model includes drift-scan effects and empirically calibrated dependences that are absent from a fixed Gaussian. We therefore use the simple response only as a transparent baseline for a falsifiable methodological question: in a controlled single-peak marginal-resolution regime, can fitting the photocentre approximation instead of a resolution-aware response produce a systematic mismatch that propagates through a joint physical orbit solution?

This paper documents the validation chain developed for that question: physical orbit conventions, the unresolved photocentre limit, the single/multi-peak boundary of the restricted surrogate, mission-grounded scan schedules, a full-sky transition experiment, and a matched-transit-count control that separates the effects of observation number from scan geometry. The frozen quantitative experiments remain baseline equal-width synthetic tests; they are not empirical Gaia calibration results and are not presented as using the later response-misspecification or posterior extensions of the code.

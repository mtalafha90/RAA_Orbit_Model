\section{Introduction}
\label{sect:intro}

Binary-star orbits are among the principal routes to dynamical stellar masses. Combining independent relative astrometry and spectroscopy is especially powerful because the sky-plane orbit constrains geometry while a double-lined spectroscopic binary (SB2) provides both line-of-sight velocity amplitudes. Gaia adds precise one-dimensional astrometric information over a mission-length time baseline \citep{GaiaMission2016}.

The broad concept of combining Gaia or Hipparcos astrometry with relative astrometry and radial velocities is established. In particular, BINARYS jointly treats Hipparcos/Gaia absolute astrometry with relative astrometry and radial velocities, including resolved cases \citep{Leclerc2023}. The present problem is narrower. Gaia's published DR3 astrometric-binary processing explicitly applied preliminary filters to reduce contamination from partially resolved double stars \citep{Halbwachs2023}. Independently, \citet{Holl2023} demonstrated that source structure and close companions can introduce scan-angle-dependent image-parameter biases.

Crucially, a scan-angle- and separation-dependent along-scan response is \emph{already available}. \citet{ElBadry2024} released \texttt{gaiamock}, whose \texttt{al\_bias\_binary} places the measured one-dimensional coordinate at the peak of the combined along-scan flux profile, following Lindegren (2022). That response is nonlinear in projected separation and light ratio and reduces to the flux-weighted photocentre only at small separation. We therefore make no claim to novel measurement physics. We verify instead that the surrogate adopted here reproduces that published response to the published solver's own convergence tolerance, while removing its two piecewise approximations: a linearisation below a tenth of a resolution element, and a hard assignment to the primary above $(3-f)$ resolution elements, which we show is systematically earlier than the exact mode-splitting boundary.

What is not established in the literature is the use of such a response as the \emph{inference} model. In \texttt{gaiamock} the resolution-aware response is used only to generate data; every fitting routine adopts a plain photocentre model on a Thiele-Innes design matrix, since the package deliberately emulates Gaia's own pipeline. The published state of the art is thus to generate with a resolution-aware response and fit with a photocentre one.

We therefore investigate a candidate methodological gap rather than make a priority claim: target-level joint inference that \emph{fits} the resolution-aware response, combining independent resolved relative astrometry, both SB2 velocity curves, and Gaia-like epoch measurements, while propagating the measurement-model choice to component masses, parallax and the Gaia-band light fraction. The immediate goal is not yet a calibrated Gaia image model. Gaia's actual PSF/LSF calibration has strong colour, time, and instrument-state dependence \citep{Rowell2021}; replacing it by a fixed Gaussian would not be physically justified. Instead, we use a deliberately transparent surrogate to ask a falsifiable question: does there exist a single-peak marginal-resolution regime in which fitting a photocentre model to data generated by a resolution-aware response produces a measurable and systematic mismatch?

This paper documents the complete validation chain developed for that question: physical orbit conventions, the unresolved photocentre limit, the mathematical multi-peak boundary of the surrogate, mission-grounded scan schedules, a full-sky transition experiment, and a matched-transit-count control that separates the effects of observation number from scan geometry.

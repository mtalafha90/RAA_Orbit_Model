\section{Discussion}
\label{sect:discussion}

\subsection{Required Response Fidelity Depends on External Information}

The central result of the response-fidelity programme is not simply that a more detailed response yields a smaller $\chi^2$. The physical consequence of response misspecification depends strongly on how well the binary orbit is constrained by independent data. Under the strong resolved-astrometry/SB2 configuration, the photocentre approximation produces coherent percent-level biases in the component masses and parallax. Replacing it with the established equal-width blended response removes almost all of the mass bias, even though $\mone$ remains measurably inconsistent with the injected finite-elongation $\mtwo$ likelihood. The strong external orbit therefore protects the masses from moderate response misspecification, whereas the Gaia-band light fraction remains more sensitive.

As the external orbit is weakened, that protection disappears. The photocentre mass biases rise to approximately $5$--$6\%$, while the equal-width response develops residual mass biases at the $1$--$1.4\%$ level. The correctly specified $\mtwo$ solution remains centered on the injected parameters but becomes noisier. The response fidelity required for accurate dynamical masses is therefore not a property of Gaia measurements alone; it depends on the information balance among Gaia, resolved astrometry, and the two RV curves.

This also explains why likelihood preference and physical bias need not track each other. A weakly constrained wrong model can move the orbit parameters to absorb part of the astrometric response error, reducing $\Delta\chi^2$ while increasing the bias in $M_1$, $M_2$, and $\varpi$. Goodness of fit alone is therefore insufficient to establish that a measurement model is safe for dynamical-mass inference.

\subsection{Light Ratio as a Sensitive Response Diagnostic}

The Gaia-band light fraction reacts more strongly to measurement-response mismatch than the component masses when the external dynamical orbit is strong. At the central point, $\mone$ reduces the mass biases below $0.1\%$ but retains a $-3.3\%$ median light-fraction bias. This is physically plausible because the external visual-SB2 data constrain the geometry and mass ratio directly, whereas $\betag$ is tied more specifically to the mapping between component separation and measured blended position.

A well-constrained visual SB2 can therefore serve not only as a mass benchmark but also as a calibration target: resolved astrometry supplies the projected component separation and the two RV curves constrain the mass ratio, leaving the Gaia epoch residuals sensitive to close-pair response parameters. An ensemble of such systems may eventually constrain response nuisance parameters empirically rather than treating every response coefficient as known a priori.

\subsection{Relation to Existing Work and the Candidate Gap}

The project does not claim novelty for any one ingredient. BINARYS and orvara establish heterogeneous astrometric/RV orbit fitting \citep{Brandt2021,Leclerc2023}; \citet{Chevalier2023} combine Gaia DR3 astrometric solutions with SB2 data; \texttt{gaiamock} provides the equal-width nonlinear blended response \citep{ElBadry2024}; \citet{Liu2024} demonstrate response-aware Gaia orbital inference for a binary asteroid; \citet{Penoyre2026} provides the more general elongated-Gaussian position/resolvability framework; and \citet{Baycroft2026} establishes Bayesian Gaia epoch-astrometry-plus-RV inference.

The remaining candidate gap is consequently narrow: a stellar target-level inference that places a physical marginal-resolution response inside the epoch Gaia likelihood while the same dynamical system is simultaneously constrained by independent resolved relative astrometry and both SB2 velocity curves. We do not claim priority for that exact intersection. The more durable contribution is the quantified response-fidelity problem: when does the photocentre approximation bias individual masses, when is a simpler resolution-aware response sufficient, and when does additional response fidelity materially change the physical inference?

\subsection{What the Surrogates and V6a Do Not Establish}

Several limitations remain essential. First, $\alpha$ and $\beta_{\rm PSF}$ are research-surrogate widths; neither 50~mas nor any sampled $\aalpha$ is a calibrated Gaia resolution scale. Second, $\mtwo$ is still an idealized elongated Gaussian rather than Gaia's calibrated, colour-, focal-plane-, window-, time-, and drift-scan-dependent PLSF \citep{Rowell2021,Rowell2026}. Third, the quantitative response results use deterministic least-squares fitting. The 100-seed matched-$\mtwo$ control shows recentering for the controlled experiment but is not a posterior-coverage study. Fourth, the response-fidelity experiments use one physical binary and one archived schedule; the supporting full-sky control demonstrates that mismatch amplitude also depends on observation count and scan geometry.

The GJ~765.2 V6a experiment closes a different validation question. It demonstrates that the Newtonian relative-astrometry+SB2 engine can recover a physically sensible real binary orbit and mass scale from legacy measurements, but it contains no Gaia epoch astrometry and therefore says nothing directly about whether $\mzero$, $\mone$, or $\mtwo$ best describes Gaia's marginal-resolution measurement response. This distinction is maintained throughout the manuscript.

\subsection{DR3 Bridge and DR4 Measurement-Level Test}

Gaia DR3 provides a useful intermediate catalogue-level layer through IPD multi-peak/scan-angle diagnostics and any available NSS photocentre orbit, but these processed quantities cannot reproduce a direct epoch-level comparison of the response hierarchy. The target-specific HIP~96656 query is therefore treated as a falsification/consistency bridge, not as a substitute for the response likelihood.

The official Gaia DR4 expected-content documentation lists lower-level products relevant to this programme, including epoch astrometry, epoch images, and double-lined RVS epoch information. Once those products are public, visual SB2s with well-constrained component geometry provide a natural target set for a measurement-level response hierarchy. Where the released image samples are genuinely multi-peaked, the scientifically appropriate extension is an image/sample-domain likelihood rather than an artificial continuation of a one-coordinate model.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sect:conclusion}

We developed and tested a joint orbit framework for resolved relative astrometry, both SB2 radial-velocity curves, and Gaia-like along-scan astrometry in the marginal-resolution regime. The main conclusions are:

\begin{enumerate}
\item The broad combination of astrometry, relative astrometry and RV data, and the nonlinear blended response itself, are prior art. The principal scientific contribution is the response-fidelity question and its propagation into physical parameters rather than a claim of a new response family.

\item In the 720-fit paired hierarchy, the photocentre model $\mzero$ produces coherent physical bias. At $\betag=0.25$, $\aalpha=1$, and $\psfelong=1.5$, the median biases are $-1.26\%$ and $-1.28\%$ in the component masses, $+0.93\%$ in parallax, and $-15.34\%$ in light fraction.

\item The published equal-width response $\mone$ removes almost all of the component-mass bias under strong independent orbital constraints, despite remaining measurably misspecified relative to $\mtwo$. At the same point its median mass biases are $-0.03\%$ and $-0.09\%$, while the light-fraction bias remains $-3.30\%$.

\item Additional finite-elongation fidelity becomes less important as $\psfelong$ approaches the $\mone$ limit. At $\betag=0.25$ and $\aalpha=1$, the median $\Delta\chi^2_{1,2}$ decreases from 129.3 at $\psfelong=1.5$ to about 5.0 at $\psfelong=3$.

\item Response fidelity required for accurate masses depends strongly on the independent resolved-astrometry/SB2 information. Weakening those constraints increases the photocentre mass bias from about $1.2\%$ to $5$--$6\%$ and the residual $\mone$ mass bias to about $1$--$1.4\%$.

\item The correctly specified $\mtwo$ fit remains centered near the truth. The 100-seed matched-$\mtwo$ control gives a median parallax bias of $+0.0167\%$ with a 16--84 percentile interval of $-0.1274\%$ to $+0.1779\%$.

\item The non-Gaia physical core has now been checked on the real visual-SB2 GJ~765.2/HIP~96656. The 110-constraint fit gives $\chi^2_\nu=1.046$, $M_1+M_2=1.5897\,M_\odot$, close to the later combined value $1.594\,M_\odot$, and an orbital parallax $35.44\pm2.24$~mas. Fixing the inconsistent legacy-header parallax of 54.27~mas worsens the fit to $\chi^2_\nu=1.673$. This validates the Newtonian visual-SB2 implementation only; it is not a Gaia-response validation.

\item Gaia DR3 provides an intermediate catalogue/IPD consistency test for HIP~96656. The externally predicted ordinary-photocentre scale is 23.44~mas when the published $V$-band component ratio is used explicitly as a temporary proxy. No target-specific DR3 value is claimed until a validated catalogue export is ingested.

\item Supporting 23\,000-fit full-sky and 11\,040-fit matched-transit experiments show that observable model mismatch depends strongly on observation count and scan geometry. These effects must accompany any transfer of the response-fidelity analysis to real sky positions.
\end{enumerate}

The next methodological step is posterior-coverage testing with response nuisance parameters, followed by measurement-level application to public Gaia epoch/image products. The principal observational question is what level of response fidelity is required to keep dynamical masses, parallax, and light ratio unbiased for the available combination of Gaia, resolved astrometry, and SB2 spectroscopy.

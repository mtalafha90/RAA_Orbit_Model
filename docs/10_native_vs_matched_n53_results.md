# 10 — Native versus matched-53-transit results

Matching all sky positions to 53 nominal transits substantially reduces the apparent sky dependence of the equal-width photocentre mismatch.

At the representative \(\beta_G=0.25\), \(a_{\rm rel,ang}/\sigma=1\) configuration:

- native-schedule sky-median \(\Delta\chi^2\): approximately 2195.39;
- matched-53 sky-median \(\Delta\chi^2\): approximately 1180.94;
- native correlation with transit count: Spearman \(\rho\simeq0.7709\);
- after matching, correlation with the original native transit count: approximately \(-0.0032\);
- ratio of matched/native mismatch versus retained-transit fraction: \(\rho\simeq0.9233\).

Residual correlations with scan-angle entropy and maximum directional gap remain after count matching. The conclusion is that transit count dominates much of the native variation, while scan geometry still contributes.

This is supporting evidence for schedule dependence; it is not used to define a physical Gaia resolution threshold. The response-fidelity mass-bias experiments therefore hold one schedule fixed when comparing M0/M1/M2.
